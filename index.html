4. Customer Analytics — Segmentation & Lifetime Value

Recency, Frequency, Monetary (RFM) modeling and customer contribution curves

Demo Data

RFM Methodology (As Formulated in Sustainability 2022 Paper)

The research identifies that retail customer profiling cannot rely solely on demographic data. Instead, behavioral metrics are derived from transaction histories to compute an RFM Score:

R
Recency

Days elapsed since the customer's last invoice. Lower recency indicates active, high-affinity customers.

F
Frequency

Total count of distinct purchase transactions. Measures loyalty and repetitive engagement.

M
Monetary

Cumulative revenue generated in GBP. Indicates total lifetime financial contribution to the business.

How to Interpret the ML Experimental Results

Critical thinking and methodological reflections for BI & Data Science students

01

Accuracy Definition

Accuracy represents the proportion of correct predictions across all evaluated customer test instances. In the paper, customer profiles were categorized into 11 business behavior buckets based on product clustering (K-Means & PCA).

02

Experimental Constraints

These scores reflect the specific hyperparameter settings, train/test split, and feature engineering choices detailed in the paper. They demonstrate model viability under this exact transactional retail schema.

03

Beyond Raw Accuracy

In real-world retail deployments, accuracy alone is insufficient. Practitioners must also consider class balance, confusion matrices, computational latency, and business costs of misclassification (false positives vs false negatives).

04

Generalization Nuances

The best model for retail forecasting varies across industries, catalog dynamics, seasonal volatility, and feature sets. Continuous re-training and monitoring are essential when deploying ML in production data warehouses.

Conceptual End-to-End Pipeline

The 6-stage architecture integrating operational data, data warehousing, BI, and ML

Step 1

Business Data

POS, CRM & Web logs (541k records from UCI Online Retail repository).

Step 2

ETL & Preprocessing

Cleaning nulls, isolating returns/cancellations, and deriving temporal hierarchies.

Step 3

Data Warehouse

Star schema with SalesFact connected to Customer, Product, Time & Location dims.

Step 4

BI Analytics

Descriptive & Diagnostic (DD-BI) ROLAP/MOLAP slicing, KPIs, and ad-hoc reports.

Step 5

Machine Learning

K-Means clustering, PCA, 8 Classification models, and ARIMA sales forecasting.

Step 6

Insights Dashboard

Executive 360° decision support, dynamic ad-hoc querying, and strategic execution.

What This Dashboard Represents

  • A front-end Business Intelligence web application prototype built for academic coursework.
  • Simulates a responsive, self-service retail analytics interface as conceptualized in the paper.
  • Faithfully presents the published ML accuracy comparison table from Table 1 of the paper.
  • Illustrates multi-dimensional OLAP slicing by Date, Country, and Product Category.

Academic Boundaries & Limitations

  • The dashboard is a static web application without a live cloud database or backend server.
  • Demonstration data numbers are simulated for educational presentation and do not represent raw real-time transactional logs.
  • The frontend does not train or run machine learning models in real time; model figures are citations.
  • No customer confidential data or private credentials are included or required.
